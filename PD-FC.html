FoodCity

FoodCity is a grocery shopping experience available across responsive web, iOS and Android. It brings together online shopping, lists, favourites and planning tools in one connected experience. I first worked on the product when the digital experience was being created from the ground up, and later led a full redesign across all three platforms.
Status: Live
01 - The challenge

Grocery shopping involves many connected tasks. Customers may browse products, return to previous purchases, save favourites, build a list or move directly toward checkout. These activities needed to work together while remaining simple enough for everyday use. The product also needed to feel consistent across web, iOS and Android while respecting the behaviour and constraints of each platform.

02 - My Role

I led the UX and UI design across responsive web, iOS and Android, creating user flows, wireframes, interface designs and prototypes. I also developed reusable patterns and components, prepared development-ready assets and worked closely with the team throughout implementation.

03 - Approach and key decisions

The experience was organised around the activities users perform most often rather than the internal structure of the business. Flows were designed to reduce unnecessary steps between browsing, selecting products, managing lists and completing an order.
Features such as favourites, lists and previous selections were treated as central parts of the experience. Shared structure, hierarchy and interaction principles helped create consistency across platforms, while platform-specific patterns were used where they improved usability.
As the product grew, we introduced and refined reusable components and design patterns to support new functionality and reduce unnecessary differences between screens and platforms.

04 - How it evolved

The first version established the foundation of the digital product. Once it was live and had matured, I returned to redesign the web, iOS and Android experiences with a broader understanding of how the product had developed.
The redesign refined the structure, interface and interaction patterns, was released across all three platforms and continued to receive improvements after launch.
